# Paper execution and accounting

This is a top-of-book, immediate-or-reject *simulation*, not a fill prediction. A buy is priced at best ask + `SLIPPAGE_BPS` × ask / 10,000; a sell at best bid - slippage. Fees are `abs(quantity × simulated price) × FEE_RATE`, charged to paper cash. If the requested size exceeds the visible top level's size, reject rather than pretending to fill deeper liquidity. Quotes are not reserved, so real exchange fills could differ or fail. The source's bookTicker sizes are aggregates, not guaranteed executable liquidity. `signal_price` is the mid-price at the decision. IDs label local paper fills only.

The ledger applies signed quantity to each position, adjusts cash for notional and fees, updates average entry for additions, recognizes gross realized P&L on a close, and marks unrealized P&L to the latest mid. Equity is cash plus the marked value of each position. Total P&L equals equity less initial virtual capital and therefore includes spread, slippage, and fees; gross realized P&L shown on a position does not include fee allocation. The display's win rate is a count of profitable closing fills after that fill's fee, not risk-adjusted performance. Drawdown is the largest decline from prior observed peak equity in a session. Equal-weight BTC/ETH buy-and-hold buys each symbol at its first observed midpoint with half the starting virtual capital; it currently ignores benchmark fees/spread, a favorable assumption that must be stated when comparing results. Each symbol starts at its own first quote; benchmark is hidden until both are present.

Fills and signals are recorded in SQL; positions and equity snapshots are sampled at most once per second to avoid storing every raw tick. The system starts a new paper session on restart rather than reconstructing an unsafe in-progress account. Session history remains in SQL, but the API exposes only the active in-memory session. The design is one process and one active session, not a distributed event-sourced accounting engine.
